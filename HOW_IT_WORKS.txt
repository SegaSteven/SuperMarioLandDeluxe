1. INSTALLING THE UPDATE
================================================================================

  1. Close Godot.
  2. Back up your project folder (or commit it in git - you already use git).
  3. Unzip the update INTO your project folder, saying YES to replace files.
     The folders inside the zip match your project (Scripts/, Scenes/, Assets/).
  4. Open the project. Godot will import the new images (takes a few seconds).
  5. Press Play.

If Godot says a script has an error the first time, use Project > Reload Current
Project once - it just needs to rebuild its list of class names.

2. THE BIG IDEAS (READ THIS FIRST)
================================================================================

The old code broke because each script was reaching into other scripts and
checking lots of separate true/false variables, all in one huge file. The new
code is built from a few simple ideas that repeat everywhere:

  A) MARIO'S BODY ONLY BUMPS INTO THE WORLD.
     Mario's CharacterBody2D collides with ground, blocks and pipes only.
     Enemies and items are noticed by his Area2D ("hitbox"). That's why
     enemies can't push Mario around, and why stomping works reliably.

  B) ENEMIES DECIDE WHAT HAPPENS TO THEM.
     When Mario touches an enemy he works out WHICH KIND of touch it was and
     calls one function on the enemy:
         enemy.stomp(mario)          - he landed on its head
         enemy.touch_player(mario)   - any other touch
         enemy.die_from_hit(dir)     - star power, block bump, explosion
         enemy.hit_by_projectile(b)  - superball or torpedo
     Each enemy script decides what those mean. A Goombo dies when stomped;
     a Nokobon goes into its shell; Pionpi just lies flat for a moment.
     So you never edit Mario to add a new enemy.

  C) MARIO IS ALWAYS IN EXACTLY ONE STATE.
     Ground, Air, Transform (growing/shrinking), Pipe, Dead, Vehicle.
     Each state is its own small script. No more "is_jumping and not
     is_dead and not in_pipe and ..." checks everywhere.

  D) SETTINGS LIVE IN THE INSPECTOR.
     Almost every number (speeds, jump height, hit points, timers, where a
     pipe goes) is an @export variable. Click the node and change it in the
     Inspector - you rarely need to open a script. Hover over a setting in
     the Inspector to read its description.

3. MARIO AND HIS STATE MACHINE
================================================================================

Files:
  Scenes/player.tscn               Mario's scene (same as before + StateMachine)
  Scripts/Player_Updated.gd        Mario's shared "tools" (class PlayerUpdated)
  Scripts/player_animated_sprite.gd  Picks small_run / big_jump etc.
  Scripts/Player/StateMachine.gd   Runs the current state
  Scripts/Player/PlayerState.gd    Base class every state extends
  Scripts/Player/States/*.gd       The states themselves

In player.tscn you'll now see:

    Player
     ├─ AnimatedSprite2D, Area2D, BodyCollisionShape, sounds... (unchanged)
     └─ StateMachine
         ├─ Ground      standing, walking, running, skidding, crouching
         ├─ Air         jumping and falling (and the bounce after a stomp)
         ├─ Transform   growing / getting the flower / shrinking (flickers)
         ├─ Pipe        sliding into a pipe
         ├─ Dead        death animation, then reload or game over
         └─ Vehicle     submarine / plane (auto-scroll levels)

Each physics frame, Player_Updated.gd asks the current state what to do. The
state uses Mario's tools and decides when to switch to another state.

MARIO'S TOOLS (in Player_Updated.gd) that states can use:
  run_movement(delta)      the normal "Mario in control" frame: gravity,
                           left/right, shooting, moving, blocks, pipes,
                           camera edge, enemies/items, animation
  apply_gravity(delta)     just gravity
  apply_horizontal(delta, input)   just left/right movement
  wants_to_jump()          true if jump was pressed in the last few frames
  start_jump()             jump (sets speed, hitbox, plays sound)
  check_contacts()         handle touching enemies and items
  hurt(source)             something hurt Mario (shrink or die)
  die()                    kill Mario
  set_mode(mode)           change size instantly (SMALL / BIG / SHOOTING)
  transform_to(mode, damage)   change size with the flicker animation
  activate_star(seconds)   star power (no number = length of the star song)
  shoot()                  throw a superball
  bounce_off_enemy()       the little hop after a stomp
  get_state_name()         e.g. &"Ground"

SETTINGS (click Player, look in the Inspector):
  Movement:  max_walk_speed, max_run_speed, walk_accel, stop_decel,
             turn_decel (skid), air_decel, block_backtracking (can't walk
             off the left of the screen, like the Game Boy)
  Jumping:   jump_velocity, gravity_rise, gravity_fall, max_fall_speed,
             jump_cut (short hop when you let go early), coyote_time (jump
             just after leaving a ledge), jump_buffer (press jump just
             before landing)
  Stomping:  stomp_bounce, stomp_bounce_held, stomp_tolerance
  Powerups:  star_duration (0 = song length), star_flash_speed,
             star_warning_time, iframe_duration (blink after being hit),
             max_superballs, pipe_center_tolerance

DAMAGE RULES:
  - Small Mario hit          -> dies
  - Big / flower Mario hit   -> shrinks (Transform state), then blinks for
                                iframe_duration seconds and can't be hurt
  - While growing/shrinking, in a pipe, or dead -> can't be hurt at all
    (each state has "allows_damage" ticked or not)
  - Pits (DeathBox) kill instantly, even with the star

4. ADDING YOUR OWN MOVE TO MARIO (CUSTOM STATES)
================================================================================

Example: a spin jump that can break blocks, a swimming state, a climbing
state for vines, a slide... Here's the recipe.

  1. Make a new script, e.g. Scripts/Player/States/swim.gd:

        extends PlayerState
        ## Swimming: tap jump to paddle upwards, sink slowly.

        @export var paddle_speed: float = -80.0
        @export var sink_speed: float = 40.0

        func enter(_msg: Dictionary) -> void:
            player.velocity.y = 0.0

        func physics_update(delta: float) -> void:
            var input := Input.get_axis("left", "right")
            player.apply_horizontal(delta, input)
            player.velocity.y = move_toward(player.velocity.y, sink_speed, 200 * delta)
            if Input.is_action_just_pressed("jump"):
                player.velocity.y = paddle_speed
            player.move_and_slide()
            player.check_contacts()
            player.animate(input)

  2. Open Scenes/player.tscn. Right-click StateMachine > Add Child Node >
     Node. Rename it "Swim" (the NAME is the state's name). Attach swim.gd.

  3. Switch into it from anywhere:
        player.state_machine.transition_to(&"Swim")
     e.g. from an Area2D "water zone" in your level:
        func _on_body_entered(body):
            if body is PlayerUpdated:
                body.state_machine.transition_to(&"Swim")
     and back out with transition_to(&"Air").

  Things a state can switch on/off in the Inspector:
     allows_contacts    can enemies/items touch Mario in this state?
     allows_damage      can Mario be hurt?
     allows_stomp       can he squash enemies by landing on them?
     allows_transform   can mushrooms/flowers change his size?
  And a function a state can override:
     on_hurt(source) -> bool    return true if you handle damage yourself
                                (the submarine uses this: one hit = death)

  You can listen to state changes:
     player.state_machine.state_changed.connect(func(old, new): print(old, " -> ", new))

5. HOW ENEMIES WORK
================================================================================

File: Scripts/Enemies/enemy.gd  (class Enemy). Every enemy extends it.

What the base class already does for you:
  - Gravity and walking (Goombo-style), turning round at walls
    (optionally at ledges too: turn_at_ledges)
  - Waking up when it comes near the screen and deleting itself when it's
    far behind the camera (no VisibleOnScreenNotifier needed any more)
  - Squashing flat (die_flat) or flipping over and falling off screen
    (die_from_hit), playing the "dead" animation and the "Enemykilled" sound
  - Giving points and showing the floating points label
  - Hurting Mario when touched

Settings on every enemy (Inspector > Behaviour / Physics / Waking up):
  score                   points for beating it
  horizontal_speed        walking speed
  start_direction         -1 walks left, 1 walks right
  turn_at_ledges          turn round instead of walking off edges
  can_be_stomped          false = touching it from above hurts too
  immune_to_projectiles   superballs/torpedoes do nothing
  immune_to_superballs    superballs pass through but torpedoes work (Honen)
  hits_to_kill            superballs/torpedoes needed (Yurarin = 2)
  sprite_faces_left       tick if your art faces left (most SML art does)
  gravity, max_fall_speed
  always_active           ignore the camera and always run
  wake_margin             how far outside the screen it wakes up
  despawn_margin          how far behind the camera before it's deleted

Animations the base class plays if they exist on the AnimatedSprite2D:
  "walk" or "default" (your choice - set autoplay), and "dead".

Sound nodes it plays if they exist: an AudioStreamPlayer named "Enemykilled".

6. MAKING A NEW ENEMY (STEP BY STEP)
================================================================================

The easiest way: DUPLICATE an existing enemy scene that behaves similarly,
swap the art and change the settings. E.g. duplicate kumo.tscn for any hopper.

From scratch:
  1. Scene > New Scene. Root node: CharacterBody2D. Name it.
  2. Add children:
       AnimatedSprite2D   - add your animations ("walk", "dead", ...)
       CollisionShape2D   - a RectangleShape2D around the body
       AudioStreamPlayer  - name it "Enemykilled", pick enemykilled.wav
  3. Attach a script. First line:  extends Enemy
     (or extends HopperEnemy / FlyerEnemy / ShooterEnemy - see section 7)
  4. Save it in Scenes/Enemies/ and drag it into a level.

That's a working walking enemy already. To make it do something different,
override one or more of these in your script:

    func _on_ready() -> void:
        # setup (use this instead of _ready, so the base setup still runs)

    func _alive_physics(delta: float) -> void:
        # how it moves every frame while alive
        apply_gravity(delta)
        velocity.x = direction * horizontal_speed
        move_and_slide()

    func stomp(player) -> void:
        # Mario landed on it. Default: die_flat()

    func touch_player(player) -> void:
        # Mario touched it. Default: player.hurt(self)

    func hit_by_projectile(ball) -> void:
        # superball/torpedo. Default: counts hits_to_kill, then die_from_hit()

    func die_from_hit(from_direction: float = 1.0) -> void:
        # star / block bump / explosion. Default: flip over and fall

Handy helpers inside an enemy script:
    get_player()              Mario (or null)
    direction_to_player()     -1 if Mario is to the left, 1 if to the right
    _face(dir)                flip the sprite to face a direction
    _play_anim("name")        play an animation if it exists
    _play_sfx("NodeName")     play a child AudioStreamPlayer if it exists
    award_score()             give points + show the points label
    die_flat() / die_from_hit()

Tip: set default numbers in _init() rather than _on_ready():
    func _init() -> void:
        score = 400
  _init runs BEFORE the Inspector values are applied, so you can still change
  them per-enemy in the Inspector. (Setting them in _on_ready would overwrite
  your Inspector changes - that was a bug in a few of the old scripts.)

7. READY-MADE ENEMY TYPES: HOPPING, FLYING, SHOOTING
================================================================================

These live in Scripts/Enemies/Templates/. Attach one to a new enemy and tune
it in the Inspector - no code needed.

--- HOPPING: hopper_enemy.gd (class HopperEnemy) ---------------------------
  Waits on the ground, then hops. Examples: Kumo, Pionpi, Fly.
    hop_speed          height of a normal hop
    big_hop_speed      0 = never; otherwise every few hops it does a big one
    hops_before_big    normal hops between big ones
    wait_on_ground     pause between hops
    air_speed          sideways speed in the air (0 = straight up and down)
    face_player        turn towards Mario before each hop
    anim_ground / anim_air   animation names

--- FLYING / SWIMMING: flyer_enemy.gd (class FlyerEnemy) -------------------
  No gravity. Underwater enemies use this too. Pick a "pattern":
    STRAIGHT         straight line (Torion fish, Bullet Biff, Chicken)
    WAVE             sideways, bobbing up and down (Batadon)
    PATROL_UP_DOWN   up and down on the spot (Gunion)
    PATROL_SIDE      left and right between two points
    CHASE            slowly homes in on Mario (Yurarin)
    DIVE             hovers, swoops down when Mario walks underneath
  Settings: fly_speed, wave_height, wave_speed, patrol_distance,
            chase_turn_speed, dive_trigger_width, dive_speed, anim_fly.
  Stomping a flyer knocks it out of the sky. Untick can_be_stomped if it
  should hurt instead.

--- SHOOTING: shooter_enemy.gd (class ShooterEnemy) ------------------------
  Fires a projectile every few seconds. Examples: Gao, Nyololin, Pompon
  Flower, Yurarin Boo, Roketon.
    projectile_scene   what it fires (boss_fireball.tscn by default)
    aim                AT_PLAYER, FORWARD, BOTH_SIDES, ALL_DIRECTIONS, UP_ARC
    fire_interval, first_shot_delay, shot_speed, shot_count (ALL_DIRECTIONS)
    arc_up_speed, arc_gravity (UP_ARC lobs things up and over)
    only_when_player_near   > 0 = only shoots when Mario is this close
    movement           NONE, WALK, FLY_LEFT, DRIFT_TO_PLAYER, BOB
    anim_idle / anim_shoot
  A child AudioStreamPlayer named "Fire" plays on each shot.

--- PROJECTILES: Bosses/boss_projectile.gd (class BossProjectile) ----------
  Used for every enemy shot, not just bosses. Hurts Mario, can't be stomped,
  superballs pass through it.
    speed, arc_gravity, throw_up_speed, pass_through_walls, lifetime

--- OTHER BUILDING BLOCKS (Scripts/Enemies/SML/) ---------------------------
  orbiter.gd        circles round a point or a node (Roto Disc, Chikako, Tamao)
  enemy_spawner.gd  keeps spawning a scene while on screen (Bullet Biff
                    cannon, Ganchan rock dropper, fish schools...)

================================================================================
9. NOKOBON (BOMB KOOPA)
================================================================================

File: Scripts/Enemies/SML/koopa.gd.

  Stomp it ........ hides in its shell, fuse starts (the shell is SAFE to touch)
  Stomp again ..... explodes straight away
  Superball ....... explodes straight away (untick superball_detonates if you'd
                    rather it just died)
  Star / block .... explodes
  The explosion ... destroys every enemy within blast_radius. Other Nokobon
                    caught in it go off too (chain reaction, chain_delay apart).
                    Hurts Mario if he's within hurt_radius.

Settings: fuse_time, explosion_time, blast_radius, hurt_radius,
          superball_detonates, chain_delay.

WHY BIG MARIO USED TO GET HIT TWICE: the explosion touched him on every frame
it existed. Shrinking started a timer, but nothing blocked damage DURING the
shrink animation, so the next frame hit him again. Now:
  - each explosion can only hurt Mario once, and
  - the Transform state ignores all damage while it plays, then the blink
    (iframe_duration) protects him afterwards.

10. BOSSES AND THE SWITCH BEHIND THE BOSS
================================================================================

Files: Scripts/Enemies/Bosses/boss.gd (class Boss)
       Scripts/Enemies/Bosses/boss_switch.gd (class BossSwitch)
       Scenes/Enemies/Bosses/example_boss.tscn, boss_switch.tscn,
       boss_fireball.tscn

HOW IT WORKS (like the Game Boy game):
  - Bosses can't be stomped (touching hurts Mario).
  - Superballs (or torpedoes) take 1 health each. King Totomesu has 5.
  - Star / block bumps / explosions also take 1 health (not an instant kill).
  - At 0 health the boss is knocked out and falls off the screen.
  - The SWITCH behind the boss: touch it and the boss is beaten instantly,
    the music stops, and the exit appears. Even if you beat the boss with
    superballs, you still touch the switch to open the exit (same as SML).

SETTING UP A BOSS ROOM:
  1. Drag a boss scene into the level (e.g. example_boss.tscn).
  2. Drag Scenes/Enemies/Bosses/boss_switch.tscn behind it.
  3. Put your exit door (door_normal) where it should be.
  4. Click the switch. In the Inspector:
       Bosses       - drag the boss in (empty = every boss in the level)
       Reveal Nodes - drag the exit door in. It stays hidden and switched off
                      until the switch is pressed.
  Done.

  For water/sky bosses with no switch (Dragonzamasu, Biokinton, Tatanga), use
  the boss's own "Reveal On Defeat" list instead - drag the exit door into it.

FINISHING THE WHOLE GAME AFTER TATANGA:
  Click Tatanga and fill in "On Defeat Go To" with your ending scene (pick the
  .tscn or paste a uid://). After "On Defeat Delay" seconds the level is marked
  beaten and the game moves there. Your castle cutscenes are the OPENING (they
  end on the title screen), so the ending scene is still to be made.

BOSS SETTINGS (click the boss):
  Boss health:   max_health, hurt_invincibility, stomp_damage (>0 = stomping
                 hurts it, for a Mario-World style boss)
  Movement:      walk_speed, patrol_distance (0 = stands still),
                 jump_interval (0 = never), jump_speed
  Hover:         hover (no gravity, floats), hover_range, hover_speed,
                 hover_sway_x
  Attack:        projectile_scene, fire_interval, fire_offset,
                 shots_per_volley, volley_gap, spread_count, spread_angle,
                 throw_velocity (lob in an arc - Hiyoihoi's boulders)
  When beaten:   reveal_on_defeat, on_defeat_go_to, on_defeat_spawn,
                 on_defeat_delay, on_defeat_finishes_level
  Animations:    anim_walk, anim_jump, anim_attack, anim_hurt
  Sounds:        child AudioStreamPlayers named "BossHit" and "BossFire"

  Signals you can connect: health_changed(current, maximum) - e.g. for a
  boss health bar - and defeated(boss).

A COMPLETELY CUSTOM BOSS: make a script that extends Boss and override
_boss_behaviour(delta). Use shoot_projectile() to fire and take_damage(n) /
defeat() if you need them:

    extends Boss
    func _boss_behaviour(delta):
        velocity.x = direction_to_player() * walk_speed   # chase Mario
        if is_on_floor() and randf() < 0.01:
            velocity.y = -jump_speed
            shoot_projectile(true)                        # aimed at Mario

11. LEVEL EXITS AND PIPES  (Destination Scene + Spawn)
================================================================================

Every door and pipe now holds its OWN destination. Click it and fill in two
boxes in the Inspector - no editing level scripts.

  Destination Scene   click the folder icon and pick the .tscn, OR paste a UID.
                      To get a UID: in the FileSystem dock right-click a scene
                      > Copy UID (it looks like uid://b3e2uv1pq2x1o).
                      UIDs keep working even if you rename/move the scene.
  Destination Spawn   x, y where Mario appears in that scene.
                      Tip: open the destination scene, move your mouse to the
                      spot - the coordinates show at the bottom of the 2D view.

DOWN PIPES (PipeD1 / PipeD2):  Destination group > destination_scene,
  destination_spawn, transition_pattern. Stand on the pipe, hold down.
  If destination_scene is EMPTY the pipe works the old way (the level
  script's _on_player_scene_over_d_1_change / _2_ decides) - so your existing
  levels keep working, and you can switch pipes over one at a time.

SIDE PIPES (PipeSide): same Destination boxes. Walk into it.
  Empty = old way (the level script's _on_player_scene_under_change).

LEVEL EXITS (door_normal.gd - used by BOTH exits):
  level_scene_path   where to go: the world map, or the bonus room for the
                     upper exit (pick the .tscn or paste a uid://)
  spawn_point        where Mario appears there. For the world map leave it at
                     (0, 0) - Mario goes back to the tile he entered from.
  exit_type          NORMAL or BONUS (both count as beating the level)
  fanfare_time       seconds of "level end" music before moving on
  Either exit marks the level beaten and saves any star coins (section 12).

  NOTE: in door_normal.tscn the trigger box (CollisionShape2D) sits ~2400px to
  the right of the door node. When placing an exit, move the CollisionShape2D
  child, not just the door.

All of this goes through ONE function, so travel works the same everywhere:
    GameManager.travel_to(scene, spawn, {"pattern": "squares", "speed": 4})
You can call it from any script (e.g. a warp zone or a cutscene).

13. THE OVERWORLD MAP (POINT-TO-POINT MOVEMENT, LIKE SUPER MARIO WORLD)
================================================================================

Files: Scripts/World_Scripts/MapNode.gd, MapPath.gd, overworld_player.gd
       Scenes/WorldScenes/map_node.tscn

The map is now made of STOPS (MapNodes) joined by PATHS (MapPaths) that you
DRAW in the editor. Mario walks along the line you drew. You never edit the
player script to add levels. world_map.tscn and pyramid_map.tscn are already
converted - look at the "MapNodes" node in each to see examples.

MAPNODE - a stop on the map (level, dock, crossroads, or a portal):
  Drag Scenes/WorldScenes/map_node.tscn into the map's MapNodes node.
    level_name          shown on the HUD; also the level's ID ("1-1")
    area_name           e.g. BIRABUTO
    level_scene_path    the level (.tscn or uid://). Empty = not a level
    spawn_point         where Mario starts INSIDE that level
    show_icon           show the level icon
    idle_animation      Mario's pose standing here
    star_coins_in_level 0-3
    Portal group:  goto_scene, goto_spawn_point, auto_goto
                   (a stop that takes you to another map, e.g. the pyramid)

MAPPATH - a route from one stop to another:
  1. Select the MapNode the path STARTS at. Add Child Node > Path2D.
     Attach Scripts/World_Scripts/MapPath.gd.
  2. Inspector: Target = the MapNode it leads to.
                Direction = the d-pad direction that starts the walk.
  3. With the path selected, use the "Add Point" tool in the toolbar to click
     out the route. Start at the MapNode (0,0). No points = straight line.
  4. That's it. Mario can walk it backwards too (opposite direction key).

  Animating the walk (the bit you wanted to control):
    point_animations   one entry per point. Entry N = the animation used while
                       walking from point N-1 to point N: "Up", "Down",
                       "Left", "Right", "Water_Up", "Water_Left"... Leave an
                       entry empty and it's picked from the direction.
    all_water          use the Water_ animations for the whole path
    speed              walking speed (pixels per second)
    smooth             follow curves instead of straight segments
    animation_player + play_animation   play an AnimationPlayer animation
                       when Mario starts the walk (a bridge appearing, etc.)

  Locking paths:
    require_source_beaten   can only walk forward once this level is beaten
    required_level          e.g. "1-2" must be beaten first
    bidirectional           untick for one-way paths
    reverse_direction       which key walks it backwards (AUTO = opposite)

  OverworldPlayer settings: start_node (first ever position), snap_to_pixels,
  debug_unlock_all (ignore locks while testing).

Coming back from a level, Mario returns to the tile he entered it from, and a
beaten level's icon changes to "complete".

14. AUTO-SCROLL SUBMARINE / PLANE LEVELS (MARINE POP, SKY POP)
================================================================================

Files: Scenes/Levels/Templates/marine_pop_template.tscn
       Scripts/Level_Scripts/auto_scroll_level.gd  (class AutoScrollLevel)
       Scripts/Core/auto_scroll_camera.gd          (class AutoScrollCamera)
       Scripts/Player/States/vehicle.gd            (Mario's Vehicle state)
       Scripts/torpedo.gd, Scenes/torpedo.tscn

TO MAKE ONE:
  1. Duplicate Scenes/Levels/Templates/marine_pop_template.tscn into
     Scenes/Levels/ and rename it (e.g. 2-3.tscn).
  2. Paint your tiles on the TileMap (it uses Birabuto.tres - swap the

     your tiles have collision.
  3. Pick music for LevelMusic.
  4. Sub art: click Player > VehicleSprite. It has animations "idle", "move",

  5. Add enemies under Enemies (Torion, Yurarin, Gunion, Honen...).
  6. Move the exit (Doors/Door_normal) to the end - remember to move its

  7. Point a MapNode at it.

What happens: as the level starts, Mario switches to the Vehicle state. The
camera scrolls right on its own. Mario moves freely in 8 directions, can't
leave the screen, fires torpedoes with the action button, and gets crushed
if the screen pushes him into a wall. Touching an enemy = lose a life.

VEHICLE SETTINGS (Player > StateMachine > Vehicle):
  move_speed, acceleration, drift (e.g. (0, 8) to sink slowly),
  screen_margin, projectile_scene (torpedo.tscn), max_projectiles,
  fire_offset, one_hit_kill (untick = big Mario shrinks instead of dying),
  crush_against_walls, vehicle_size (collision box), sprite_node.
  NOTE: those settings are on the Vehicle node inside player.tscn, so they
  change it for every level. To change them for ONE level, right-click the
  Player in that level > Editable Children, then edit Vehicle there.

CAMERA SETTINGS (Camera2D in the level):
  scroll_speed, start_delay, vertical_speed (levels that scroll up/down),
  speed_zones (list of Vector2(x, new_speed): when the screen reaches x the
  speed changes; 0 = stop there, e.g. for a boss), and Limit Right = where
  the scrolling stops for good.
  From code: $Camera2D.stop_scrolling() / resume_scrolling().

SKY POP: same template - change the background, use the Sky Pop sprite,
use Roketon / Chicken / Biokinton, and give the plane a bullet scene instead
of the torpedo if you like (any scene with torpedo.gd works).

================================================================================
17. THE DEBUGGER / MEMORY BUG - WHAT IT WAS
================================================================================

The errors piling up in the debugger (and the memory climbing) came from code
that ran EVERY FRAME (60 times a second) and created something new each time:

  1. ui.gd: _process() called .connect() on three signals every frame.
     Connecting an already-connected signal is an error, so Godot logged
     3 errors x 60 frames = 180 errors per second, forever. THIS was the big
     one. Signals now connect once in _ready().
  2. letter_screen.gd: started a new 10-second timer every frame; after 10
     seconds it tried to change scene 60 times a second.
  3. Flashing_Text.gd: started a new 3-second timer every frame (about 180
     paused functions alive at any moment).
  4. Pyramid_player.gd / old fly.gd / old bun_bun.gd / old overworld_player.gd:
     "await create_timer(...)" inside _process, same problem.
  5. Old tile scripts re-emitted a "level finished" signal every frame.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER: in _process / _physics_process, never call .connect(),
never "await get_tree().create_timer()", never create_tween() unless it's
behind an if that only happens once. Use a float that counts down instead:
    var timer := 2.0
    func _process(delta):
        timer -= delta
        if timer <= 0.0:
            timer = 2.0
            do_the_thing()

21. SECRET EXITS, HIDDEN PATHS AND SECRET LEVELS
================================================================================

A secret exit opens a hidden path on the map, Super Mario World style. Unlocks
are just names you make up, like "star_road_1".

IN THE LEVEL - the secret exit:
  Use a normal exit (door_normal). Set Exit Type = SECRET and Unlocks =
  star_road_1 (several: "star_road_1, powerup_bunny"). It still counts as
  beating the level.

ON THE MAP - the hidden path and level:
  1. Add the secret level's MapNode. Set Required Unlock = star_road_1.
     It stays invisible until then.
  2. Draw a MapPath to it from a normal level (section 13). Set its Required
     Unlock = star_road_1 too. It can't be walked, either way, until then.
  3. Optional on the path: Reveal Nodes (a TileMap layer with the path tiles,
     a bridge...) - hidden until unlocked. Draw Dots = show the route as dots.

  The first time you come back to the map after unlocking, the path draws
  itself dot by dot (input paused), then the level icon appears.

From code: OverworldMan.unlock("star_road_1"), OverworldMan.is_unlocked(...).
Unlocks are saved with the rest of the progress.

22. POWER-UPS AND UNLOCKING THEM
================================================================================

The whole list is ONE file: Scripts/Player/PowerUps.gd.

  Power-up          Mario mode   Unlocked at start   Unlocked by
  Super Mushroom    BIG          yes                 -
  Superball Flower  SHOOTING     yes                 -
  Fire Flower       FIRE         no                  RED switch
  Bunny Ears        BUNNY        no                  PINK switch
  Speed Shoes       (timed)      no                  BLUE switch

  Fire Flower  - throws fireballs that bounce along the ground and burn out
                 when they hit a wall (the superball ricochets instead).
  Bunny Ears   - hold jump while falling to float down slowly (the BunnyFloat
                 state). Super Mario Land 2's carrot.
  Speed Shoes  - for 10 seconds (like the star) Mario runs twice as fast and
                 leaves a blue ghost trail. Stacks with the star.

Items (Scenes/PowerUps/): fire_flower.tscn (red), carrot.tscn,
speed_shoes.tscn (blue). One script for all of them: power_up_item.gd -
set Power Up Id, and Movement (STAY / WALK / BOUNCE).

LOCKED POWER-UPS: put the item in a ? block's Spawned Scene as normal. Until
it's unlocked, the block gives a mushroom (small Mario) or Superball Flower
instead. An item placed straight in a level always works.

Placeholder art: until you draw frames named fire_idle, bunny_run,
bunny_float etc., Mario uses his big frames tinted (the "tint" in
PowerUps.gd). Getting hit with any power-up drops Mario to small.

ADDING YOUR OWN POWER-UP (e.g. a cape):
  1. Add CAPE at the END of the PlayerMode enum in Player_Updated.gd.
  2. Add an entry to LIST in PowerUps.gd (copy "bunny" and change it).
  3. Duplicate Scenes/PowerUps/fire_flower.tscn, set Power Up Id = "cape".
  4. Its ability = a new Mario state (section 4). Name it in air_hold_state,
     or switch to it from your own code.
  5. To make a switch unlock it: "switch": "blue"/"red"/"pink", or add a new
     colour to SWITCH_COLORS.

From code: mario.give_power_up("fire"), PowerUps.unlock("bunny"),
PowerUps.is_unlocked("speed_shoes").

23. SWITCH PALACES, COLOUR SWITCHES AND COLOUR BLOCKS
================================================================================

Like Super Mario World. Three colours: BLUE (speed shoes), RED (fire flower),
PINK (bunny ears).

THE LEVELS: Scenes/Levels/SwitchPalaces/switch_palace_blue.tscn, _red, _pink
(and Templates/switch_palace_template.tscn to make more). Each has the big
switch, some outline blocks, a floor to replace with your tiles, and uses
basic_level.gd (a general level script you can use for any new level).

THE SWITCH (Scenes/Blocks/color_switch_<colour>.tscn): Mario jumps on it ->
  1. that colour's blocks everywhere turn on (saved),
  2. that colour's power-up is unlocked,
  3. the music stops and a message box pops up. It says the switch is
     active and blocks have changed - NOT what it does. The player finds out.
     (Edit the switch's Message to change the words; %s = the colour name.)
  4. when the player presses jump, the level counts as finished and the game
     goes back to the map.

ON THE MAP: on the palace's MapNode set Switch Color. The tile shows a
placeholder switch; once pressed it's drawn flat and the level CAN'T be
entered again. Normal levels stay replayable. (Replayable = false does the
same for any one-time level.) Put it behind a secret exit (section 21) so
players have to find it.

COLOUR BLOCKS (Scenes/Blocks/color_block_<colour>.tscn and
color_question_<colour>.tscn), placed in ANY level. In the Inspector:
  Color Name   blue / red / pink
  Kind         SOLID    - a plain block once switched on
               QUESTION - a ? block giving that colour's power-up once
  Show Outline untick to make it invisible until switched on
Before the switch: a dotted outline - Mario and enemies pass through.
After: solid. Use them for bridges over pits, shortcuts, walls that block
enemies, or ? blocks with the new power-up.

26. TIME BONUS AT THE END OF A LEVEL
================================================================================

Like Super Mario Land: the moment Mario touches the exit door, the clock
FREEZES. The level-clear music plays, then the clock counts down to 000 and
every unit left is added to the score (10 points each, same as the Game Boy
game), with the timerscore.wav sound playing while it counts. Then Mario
leaves the level.

Pressing the boss switch does the same: the clock freezes straight away, and
after the boss has fallen (Time Bonus Delay, 1.5 s) the time is counted into
the score. The exit door that appears afterwards finds 000 left, so it just
plays the fanfare and goes.

All of the counting lives in the level UI (Scripts/ui.gd). Click the UI node
in a level and look under "Time Bonus":
  Points Per Time Unit      10
  Tally Units Per Second    how fast it counts (130 = 400 takes about 3 s)
  Tally Sound               empty = res://Assets/SFX/timerscore.wav
  Restart Sound Each Unit   tick this (with bonustime_stagecomplete.wav as
                            the sound) if you want a "blip" for every unit

On the exit door (door_normal.gd):
  Time Bonus                untick to skip the count for this door
  Count During Fanfare      tick = count while the music plays, not after
  Pause After Count         short wait at 000 before leaving

On the boss switch (boss_switch.gd):
  Time Bonus, Time Bonus Delay

From your own scripts:
  var ui := UI.find(get_tree())
  ui.stop_time()                  # freeze the clock
  await ui.tally_time_bonus()     # count it into the score, wait until done

Note: the clock ticks once a second (the Timer node in UI.tscn). In the Game
Boy game one unit is about 2/3 of a second; set that Timer's Wait Time to
0.667 if you want it to match.


================================================================================
27. MARIO'S SPEED AND KEEPING MOMENTUM THROUGH POWER-UPS
================================================================================

SPEED: running is now 90 (was 120) and walking stays 60. At 60 frames a
second that's 1.5 px and 1 px a frame, which is roughly how fast Mario moves
on the Game Boy. Change them on the Player node: Movement > Max Walk Speed /
Max Run Speed. (The speed shoes still double whatever these are.)

MOMENTUM: getting a power-up (or shrinking from a hit) no longer stops Mario.
The Transform state (Scripts/Player/States/transform.gd) now keeps moving him
while he flickers: he carries on running, keeps rising or falling through a
jump, and you can still steer and do a short hop by letting go of jump. The
flicker shows the run/jump/stand frame of each size, so it looks right while
moving.

If you'd rather have the NES-style freeze, click Player > StateMachine >
Transform and tick Freeze On Power Up (and/or Freeze On Damage). Mario then
stops in place while flickering and carries on with exactly the speed he had
afterwards, instead of dropping.

31. CLIMBING ANIMATIONS, POWER-UP ANIMATION SETS AND PALETTE SWAPS
================================================================================

MARIO'S ANIMATIONS (Player > AnimatedSprite2D > SpriteFrames panel)
  small_   idle run jump turn down climb
  big_     idle run jump turn down climb
  shooting_ (superball) idle run jump turn down climb  + shoot, death
  fire_    idle run jump turn down climb  (copies of the SHOOTING frames)
  bunny_   idle run jump turn down climb  + bunny_float
  speed_   idle run jump turn down climb  (speed shoes, not for small Mario)
The new ones start as copies of the big frames (climb = the jump frame),
except fire_, which copies the superball's shooting_ frames: Fire Mario is the
superball Mario recoloured with the fire palette (and uses the same "shoot"
throwing pose).
Swap in your own frames whenever you like - nothing else needs changing.

WHICH ONE PLAYS
  Climbing a ladder or vine plays <set>_climb for whatever Mario is:
    small -> small_climb, mushroom -> big_climb, Fire Flower -> fire_climb,
    bunny ears -> bunny_climb, speed shoes on -> speed_climb,
    superball -> shooting_climb.
  It only animates while he's moving and pauses on the frame when he stops.
  If an animation is missing it falls back: <set>_climb -> big/small_climb
  -> the jump frame.
  Speed shoes: while they're on, speed_<action> is tried first (big sizes
  only), then the power-up's own set.

PALETTE SWAP (Player > AnimatedSprite2D > Inspector > Palette Swap)
  Recolours the frames for each power-up so the copies look different until
  you draw your own. Each list has 6 colours: [cap & shirt, overalls,
  overall shading] for big Mario's frames, then the same 3 for the superball
  (shooting) frames:
    Palette From    big:       red e6131a, blue 457db0, dark 173550
                    superball: blue 312caf, green 43b102, dark green 2c680c
    Palette Fire    orange e85a00, overalls blue 457db0 / 173550 (both halves)
    Palette Bunny   pink   ff6fbf
    Palette Speed   blue   2a6cf5, light-blue overalls
  The superball itself has no palette, so it keeps its own blue and green.
  Click a colour to change it. Palette Swap On unticked = no recolouring.
  Palette Only Actions: e.g. just "climb" to recolour only the climb frames.
  Palette Extra: more power-ups, e.g. "cape" -> [yellow, ...].
  Palette Tolerance: how close a pixel must be to count (0.06 is fine for
  pixel art).
  The shader is res://Shaders/palette_swap.gdshader. Star flashing and
  fades still work on top of it. With a palette set, the old whole-sprite
  tint from PowerUps.gd isn't used for that power-up.

  Once you've drawn real frames for a power-up, either untick Palette Swap
  On, or empty that power-up's palette list.

32. CHECKPOINTS
================================================================================

Scene: res://Scenes/checkpoint.tscn   Script: Scripts/checkpoint.gd

WHAT IT DOES
  Mario walks through a checkpoint -> it becomes his restart point. If he loses
  a life, the level starts again AT THE CHECKPOINT instead of the beginning,
  even if he died down a pipe afterwards (he's taken back to the level the
  checkpoint is in). He lands on the floor there: if the spot is inside the
  ground, a block or a pipe, he's lifted out and stands on top of it.
  The checkpoint is forgotten when he goes back to the map or finishes the
  level. Going down pipes and through doors inside the level keeps it.
  Several in one level: the one furthest RIGHT that he's touched wins, so
  walking back past an earlier one doesn't move him backwards.

ADDING ONE TO A LEVEL
  1. Drag res://Scenes/checkpoint.tscn into the level (your levels keep them
     under a "Checkpoints" node).
  2. Move it to where Mario should restart.
  3. Make the trigger tall so he can't jump over it - scale the checkpoint
     (your 1-1 ones use Scale y = 6.44) or its Area2D > CollisionShape2D.
  It's invisible in the game. In the editor it shows a little flag (yellow;
  green once it's the active one when Show In Game is on) and a blue dot at
  the restart point.

INSPECTOR
  Spawn Offset    restart point relative to the checkpoint (0,0 = right on it)
  Only Forward    don't replace a checkpoint further right (on by default)
  Show In Game    draw the flag while playing (for testing)
  Reach Sound     optional sound when it's touched
  Spawnpoint      old setting: active as soon as the level starts
  Signal "reached" fires when it becomes the restart point.

FROM CODE (GameManager)
  has_checkpoint(), clear_checkpoint()
  checkpoint_scene / checkpoint_position   where Mario will restart
  respawn_player()   puts Mario at the checkpoint (level scripts call it)

NOTE for 1-1: Checkpoint2 sits inside the green pipe at x 824, so Mario
restarts standing on the pipe. Move it a little left/right if you'd rather
he started on the ground.

================================================================================

Press Pause in a level: the game freezes (Mario, enemies, the TIME counter -

root node (the level script) to give that level its own.

Import settings. It plays from the start each time you pause and stops when you
unpause; the level music carries on from where it was.

HOW IT'S WIRED: every level already calls swap_pause_state() when Pause is
pressed (in its _input). That's in Scripts/Core/LevelData.gd and now opens

The animation settings are on the box (Scripts/UI/options_menu.gd):
grow_steps (3), grow_step_time (0.05 s), start_size (12 x 12).

39. COMING OUT OF A PIPE, AND THE START FLASH
================================================================================

COMING OUT OF A PIPE (Scripts/Player/States/pipe_exit.gd - the PipeExit state)
When Mario goes through a pipe, he comes out the other end the same way he went
in: once the screen fade has finished he slides up out of the pipe (16 px over
1 second, the same as going in), then you can move.
  - Each pipe (PipeD1, PipeD2, PipeSide) has Exit Animation in the Inspector,
    under Destination:
      Auto   - up out of the pipe if he lands on a pipe (a pipe node or pipe
               tiles). If he arrives in mid-air (dropping into an underground
               room) or on a platform like the Fallwait Block, there's no
               animation - he just drops in like before.
      Up / Down / Left / Right - always slide out that way.
      None   - no animation.
  - Set Destination Spawn to where Mario should END UP (standing on top of the
    pipe). He starts inside the pipe and slides out to there. If the spawn is a
    bit low and inside the pipe itself, he's lifted to the top of it first
    (1-3's two exits were inside their pipes and Mario used to get stuck).
  - The part of Mario still inside the pipe is cut off at the pipe's opening,
    so it works for pipes drawn with tiles too.
  - It doesn't play after losing a life (Mario starts at the checkpoint).

THE START FLASH
The black flash at the start of a level (Camera2D/Flash) is moved onto its own
screen layer when the level starts and stretched over the whole screen, so it
covers everything even when the camera is held at the left edge of the level
(it used to be off to one side if you died near the start). Edit it in the
level as before - colour etc. - the size and position don't matter any more.

40. CAMERA AREAS: MAIN (RED), SKY (BLUE) AND CAVE (GREEN)
================================================================================

Scenes/Camera/main_cam_area.tscn  - MainCamArea  (red outline)
Scenes/Camera/sky_cam_area.tscn   - SkyCamArea   (blue outline)
Scenes/Camera/cave_cam_area.tscn  - CaveCamArea  (green outline)
(scripts in Scripts/Camera/)

They're boxes you place and resize in the 2D editor. Only their EDGES are
coloured - no tint over the level - and they don't show in the game.

THE RULES
  Red - MainCamArea (one per level)
    - The normal camera area. Inside it the camera works like it always has:
      it follows Mario left and right (keeping him near the middle) and stops
      at the red box's edges. The red box IS the camera limits now - you don't
      need to type limits on the Camera2D any more.
    - Falling off the BOTTOM of the red box loses a life (the death line),
      like the original game - unless there's a green box below that spot.
  Blue - SkyCamArea (a secret above)
    - Walking past it does nothing, so it stays secret.
    - Overlap it over the red box's TOP edge by about 16 pixels.
    - When Mario, going up, gets within 3 pixels of the main camera's top
      edge while touching the blue box, the camera switches to it: it glides
      fully up, so Mario is at the bottom of the screen, and stays put. It
      can't show anything outside the blue box.
    - No death line: if Mario falls he drops back into the red area. The
      camera glides back as he falls within 3 pixels of the blue box's bottom
      edge.
  Green - CaveCamArea (a secret below)
    - Overlap it over the red box's BOTTOM edge by about 16 pixels.
    - The same going down: within 3 pixels of the main camera's bottom edge
      while touching the green box (e.g. falling down a hole), the camera
      glides fully down, so Mario is at the top of the screen, and stays put.
      Going back up within 3 pixels of the green box's top edge glides back.
    - Over a green box, the death line moves to the BOTTOM of the green box -
      so falling down the hole takes him into the cave instead of killing him,
      and falling out of the bottom of the cave loses a life.
  Going back
    - Walking out of the side of a blue / green box also glides back to normal.
    - Which way Mario is moving decides it, so the camera never flickers
      between two areas.
    - Two blue (or two green) boxes side by side: walking from one into the
      other switches straight across.
    - A blue / green box taller than one screen: within 3 pixels of that
      screen's edge moves the camera on another full step the same way.
  Every switch is one smooth glide (0.6 seconds, easing in and out): the
  camera moves in a straight line - diagonally if it has to - from where it is
  to its spot in the new area, and the new area's edges only apply once it
  gets there. On the way it can show a little outside the boxes; cover that
  with sprite art. Mario always stays on screen while it glides, at any speed.
  DeathBoxes still work as before - keep them for pits that aren't at the very
  bottom of the screen. Remove the DeathBox over a hole that leads to a cave.
  DeathBoxes (Scenes/death_box.tscn) show as DARK RED boxes in the editor so
  they're easy to spot (and in the game with Debug > Visible Collision Shapes).

HOW TO
  1. Each level (1-1, 1-2, 1-3) already has a MainCamArea, set to the camera
     limits it had. To change where the camera stops, select MainCamArea and
     drag its edges (or set Position / Size in the Inspector).
  2. A secret above: drag Scenes/Camera/sky_cam_area.tscn into the level. Put
     it over the top of the red box, overlapping the red box's top edge by
     about 16 pixels (the overlap is where Mario touches it to switch).
     Make it at least one screen big (256 x 144). Build the secret inside it
     (above y 0) and give Mario a way up (a vine block...).
  3. A secret below: drag Scenes/Camera/cave_cam_area.tscn in, under the bottom
     of the red box, overlapping its bottom edge by about 16 pixels, at least
     256 x 144. Make a hole in the ground above it, delete any DeathBox over
     that hole, and build the cave inside the green box.
  4. A new level: drag Scenes/Camera/main_cam_area.tscn in and size it to the
     whole level (top at y 0, bottom at y 144 for the normal screen height).
     Levels without one (like the underground rooms) still use the Camera2D's
     own limits.

INSPECTOR
  MainCamArea   Switch Margin  how close (pixels) to the edge Mario has to get
                               to switch (3 - try 2 to 4)
                Move Time      seconds the camera's glide takes - smaller is
                               faster (0.6)
                Death Line     falling off the bottom loses a life
  CaveCamArea   Death At Bottom  falling out of its bottom loses a life
  All of them   Edge Width     how thick the coloured outline is

41. CHECKPOINT SPRITE, STAR COIN BOXES AND THE MAP'S COINS
================================================================================

CHECKPOINT SPRITE (Scenes/checkpoint.tscn, Scripts/checkpoint.gd)
Each checkpoint now has an AnimatedSprite2D child called Sprite, with
placeholder frames in Assets/Sprites/Checkpoint/ - swap in your own. It uses
three animations:
  idle      - before Mario touches it (can loop)
  activate  - plays ONCE when he touches it (keep Loop off)
  active    - the 1-frame sprite it switches to afterwards. It stays like
              this after losing a life (Mario restarts there).
  - A checkpoint with Spawnpoint ticked is the START of the level, so its
    sprite is hidden.
  - A checkpoint is now just a MARKER on the ground - no trigger box, nothing
    to stretch. Mario gets it as soon as he passes its x position, at any
    height (even over the top of the screen). In the editor a dashed line shows
    that point, labelled CHECKPOINT (or START for a Spawnpoint one).
  - Level start: tick Spawnpoint - it's active from the moment the level starts
    (unless Mario has reached a later one), sprite hidden, line says START.
  - The checkpoints in 1-1, 1-2 and 1-3 sit on the ground, unstretched.
  - Different animation names? Change them under Sprite in the checkpoint's
    Inspector (Anim Idle / Anim Activate / Anim Active).

STAR COIN BOXES (the level script, Scripts/Level_Scripts/basic_level.gd)
Select a level's root node: under "Star Coins" in the Inspector there are
three boxes, Star Coin 1, Star Coin 2 and Star Coin 3. Drag the level's coins
from the Scene dock into them - the box a coin is in decides whether it's the
1st, 2nd or 3rd coin (it overrides the coin's own Coin Number), and that's the
order the map shows them in.
  - When the level runs, the Output panel warns if a box is empty, if one
    coin is in two boxes, or if a coin in the level isn't in a box.
  - A coin in an underground room goes in THAT room's boxes. If a level's
    coins are spread over its rooms, untick Check Star Coins so it doesn't
    warn about the boxes that are empty on purpose. (Underground rooms and
    switch palaces have it unticked already.)
  - Your levels don't have any star coins yet, so they warn "box is empty"
    until you add them.

THE MAP'S COINS (Scripts/World_Scripts/world_ui.gd)
The map HUD only shows the star coins you've COLLECTED in the level Mario
stands on, each in its own place: get coins 1 and 3 and those two show, with
a gap where coin 2 goes. Tick Show Missing Coins on the WorldUI node to show
hollow coins in the places of the ones still to find. How many places there
are is still set by Star Coins In Level on each MapNode.

42. SCORE POP-UPS, PIRANHA PLANTS, SLABS, LIFTS AND GANCHAN
================================================================================

SCORE POP-UPS (Scenes/points_label.tscn, Scripts/points_label.gd)
The points that show when Mario kills an enemy - any way: stomp, superball,
star, kick - float straight up 32 pixels, then vanish. White text with a black
shadow 1 pixel down and to the right (LabelSettings in points_label.tscn). Coins and star coins use
the same label. Rise (32) and Rise Time (0.8 s) are on the script.

PIRANHA PLANTS (Scripts/Enemies/SML/parana_plant.gd)
  - They won't come out while Mario is inside their WATCH AREA: a box around
    the pipe - Watch Width 48 (the pipe + 16 each side), from Watch Above 40
    pixels over the pipe's top down to Watch Below 32 under it. So standing
    next to the pipe, on it, or jumping just over it keeps the plant in.
    Tick Show Watch Area to see the box in the game.
  - They pop out at random: each wait inside the pipe is a random time
    between Wait Min (1.5 s) and Wait Max (4 s), and every plant starts on its
    own random timer. Rise Time = how fast it comes out (smaller = faster).
  - Superballs kill them (100 points).

FALLING SLABS (1-3: Scenes/FallingSlab.tscn, Scripts/falling_slab.gd)
When Mario walks under one it shakes for Drop Delay (1 s), then falls ALL the
way down and off the screen, slowly (Fall Gravity 100, Max Fall Speed 43.5) - it doesn't stop if he moves away. Landing on Mario
hurts him (small Mario loses a life); tick Kill Instantly to always kill. It
falls through the ground and pipes on purpose, so it stays an Area2D. It's
drawn in front of the pipes (z_index 3).

HIDDEN BLOCKS AND LIFTS (1-3)
  - A hidden block now shows itself AND gives its item in the same bump (it
    used to only show itself, so 1-3's lifts never came out).
  - The lift (Scripts/lift_Platform.gd) is an AnimatableBody2D moved with the
    physics, so Mario rides it properly - e.g. running against the wall next to
    1-3's first lift keeps him on it. It rises at Speed (-10 = 10 px/s up),
    carrying Mario up onto the top of the level. Once it's out of the top of
    the main camera area (MainCamArea) it vanishes - if Mario is still on it,
    he drops back down.
  - 1-3's lift at the very start (x 20) works from below. The one at x 1204 is
    on top of a brick: break the brick (Super Mario) to reach it.

GANCHAN (Scripts/Enemies/SML/ganchan.gd) - Easton's boulders
  - Enemy: touching its side hurts Mario. Can't be destroyed (a star smashes it).
  - Platform: it's solid on top - Mario can stand on it and ride it as it rolls
    and bounces.
  - Crush: if it comes down on Mario it hurts him (Crush Kills = always kill).
  - Hiyoihoi (the Easton boss) throws them - the same Ganchan scene, so his
    work the same way. Settings: Rideable, Side Hurts, Crush Kills.

43. THE TEST LEVEL (AND RUN ANIMATION TIMING)
================================================================================

RUN ANIMATION (unchanged): small_run, big_run, bunny_run and speed_run are 15
frames a second, fire_run and shooting_run 10, sped up with Mario's speed
(speed / 40, between 0.6x and 3x) in Scripts/player_animated_sprite.gd. So
walking (60 px/s) = 1.5x and running with the button held (90 px/s) = 2.25x:
small / big 22.5 fps walking, 33.75 fps running.

TEST LEVEL: Scenes/Levels/Test/test_run.tscn - 4000 pixels of flat ground with
a tick every 16 px along the ground and height rulers every 512 px (marked
every 8 px, numbered every 16). Open it and press F6 to try Mario's running
and jumping. Measured there: a tap jump is 16 px high, a full jump 48 px
(3 tiles of 16), the same with a run-up. Delete the Test folder whenever you
like - nothing else uses it.

45. THE FAKE DAISY CUTSCENE (END OF A CASTLE LEVEL)
================================================================================

Scene: res://Scenes/Cutscene/fake_daisy.tscn   Script: Scripts/Cutscene/fake_daisy.gd
Reusable - drop one at the end of 1-3, 2-3, 3-3 (or anywhere else).

WHAT HAPPENS (like the original):
  1. Mario lands on the BossSwitch: Mario, the enemies and their shots all
     freeze like the game is paused while the level end music plays
     (the switch's Freeze Sound: Assets/Audio/12 Level End.mp3); once it's finished everything on screen
     blows up (section 46). Then the time left is counted into the score, and
     once that's done (Start Delay, 0.5 s, later) the Door goes up (its
     "opening" animation + doorway.wav). Mario stays frozen until the door is
     all the way up and through the pan, then unfreezes (drops off the button)
     and walks in.
  2. The camera pans over to the fake Daisy room - the PINK DaisyCamArea
     (Pan Delay 0.3 s after the door, Pan Time 0.75 s) - and stays there,
     centred on the gap between Mario (where he stops) and Daisy, so Daisy is
     just off centre (Pan To; Pan Offset nudges it).
  3. Once the pan has finished (Walk After Pan), the Princess Daisy (credits)
     music starts and Mario
     walks in by himself from wherever he is - left or right of her, however
     far - and stops Stop Distance (28 px) before Daisy, facing her.
     MUSIC TIMING: the credits song switches at 8 s, so Daisy changes exactly
     when the music reaches Change At Music Time (8 s). Mario walks faster if he
     has to (up to Max Walk Speed, 90 = his run) so the message, the pauses and
     her arms-out pose fit in before then; if he's early she waits for the 8 s.
     Untick Sync To Music to just use Walk Speed and the pauses.
  4. OH! DAISY types out letter by letter at the top of the screen.
  5. Daisy holds her arms out; the music stops the moment her sprite changes,
     and she flashes into the monster (daisytransform.wav) as the faker music
     (Change Music: Assets/Audio/Original/Oh! Daisy.wav) plays once.
  6. The monster hops off the screen: one hop a second, 36 px high, 24 px along.
     The level waits for the faker music to end before leaving.
  7. Finish Delay later the level counts as beaten and the wipe goes to the map.

SETTING ONE UP:
  1. Instance fake_daisy.tscn. Its origin is the bottom of the door's opening.
  2. Door Position / Daisy Position move the door and Daisy (or right-click the
     node > Editable Children and drag them). In the editor a pink dashed line
     shows where Mario stops.
  3. Boss Switch: drag the level's BossSwitch in (don't also put an exit door in
     its Reveal Nodes - the cutscene ends the level).
  4. Add res://Scenes/Camera/daisy_cam_area.tscn (pink outline), stretch it
     around the room (at least 256 x 144) and drag it into Daisy Cam Area.
     While the cutscene has the camera, the MainCamArea's switching and death
     line are off, and Mario may be off screen until he walks in.
  5. Monster Scene: the enemy Daisy turns into - any enemy .tscn (fly.tscn by
     default). Only its look is used; they all hop the same way. Or set Monster
     Frames + Monster Animation.

EVERYTHING ADJUSTABLE (Inspector groups):
  Setup     Boss Switch, Daisy Cam Area, Door Position, Daisy Position, Show Guides
  Timing    Start Delay, Door Open Speed, After Door Pause, Before Message Pause,
            After Message Pause, Arms Out Time, After Transform Pause, Finish Delay
  Camera    Pan Camera, Pan Delay, Pan Time, Pan Smooth, Walk After Pan (Mario
  pan       waits for the pan), Pan To (between Mario and Daisy / Daisy / middle
            of the pink box), Pan Offset
  Mario     Walk Speed, Stop Distance, Follow Floor (keeps his feet on slopes /
            steps), Face Daisy
  Message   Message, Seconds Per Letter, Letter Sound, colour, Font Size, Shadow
            (colour / offset), Message Position, Message Align, Message Stays
  Daisy     idle / arms animation names, Daisy Faces Left
  Monster   Monster Scene / Frames / Animation, Monster Flip, Flashes, Flash Time,
            Hop Height, Hop Distance, Hop Direction (1 right / -1 left), Hop Every,
            Hop Air Time, Hop Count (0 = until off screen)
  Sounds    Door Sound, Music, Music Volume, Music Starts (door opens / pan
            starts / Mario walks / Mario stops / message), Music Stops On Change,
            Change Music (the faker music) + volume, Wait For Change Music,
            Sync To Music, Change At Music Time, Max Walk Speed,
            Jingle (extra sound as the message starts, empty), Transform Sound,
            Hop Sound, SFX Volume
  Finishing Counts As Win, Go To, Spawn Point, Unlocks, Transition Pattern / Speed
  Signals: door_opened, mario_arrived, transformed, finished. start() runs it.

YOUR ART (placeholders now):
  Door/AnimatedSprite2D  closed / opening (plays once) / open - placeholders in
                         Assets/Sprites/FakeDaisy/door_*.png, 16x32.
  Daisy                  idle and arms_out (Assets/Cutscenes/Daisy_idle.png,
                         Daisy_arms.png).

46. THE BOSS SWITCH BLOWS EVERYTHING UP
================================================================================

Pressing the BossSwitch (Scenes/Enemies/Bosses/boss_switch.tscn) now blows up
every enemy on the screen - bosses too, and all their shots (fireballs,
bubbles, spears, boulders...). Each turns into a little Blast and gives its
points (its own Score; a boss gives its Score too). Shots and anything else
worth 0 give Shot Points (100). A boss off the screen is still beaten and falls,
like before.

ORDER: Mario touches it -> Mario, enemies and shots freeze, like the game is
paused, for exactly as long as Freeze Sound (12 Level End.mp3, the level end music)
plays - that's the switch's only sound ->
once it's finished it all blows up ("blasted") -> the time bonus is
counted into the score (Time Bonus Delay 0.5 s, then the count) -> "done" (the
FakeDaisy starts from it). Mario stays frozen (Hold Mario) until the cutscene
walks him in; with no cutscene connected he gets his controls back straight
after the count. He can't be hurt while frozen.

PRESSING IT: Mario has to land on top of it (jump onto it) - walking into its
side does nothing (untick Press From Above Only to allow that). He freezes
standing on it and drops back to the floor when the cutscene walks him in.

Settings on the BossSwitch: Press From Above Only; group Freeze: Freeze Sound,
Freeze Time (0 = the freeze is just the sound's length; only used to make it
longer or if there's no sound), Hold Mario.
Settings on the BossSwitch, group Blow Up:
  Blow Up On Screen  on/off
  Blast Scene        res://Scenes/Effects/blast.tscn
  Shot Points        100
  Blast Stagger      seconds between each blast (0 = all at once)
  Screen Margin      counts as on screen this far past the edges (8 px)
  Blast Sound        nokobomb.wav (plays once)

YOUR ART (8 x 8 placeholders, like a block):
  The switch   Assets/Sprites/BossSwitch/switch_up.png and switch_pressed.png
               (AnimatedSprite2D animations "default" and "pressed").
  The blast    Assets/Sprites/Blast/blast_1..4.png (blast.tscn, animation
               "default", plays once at 12 fps, then it's removed).
For code: any enemy has blow_up(points, blast_scene); a Boss's blow_up also
does everything defeat() does (signal, Reveal On Defeat, On Defeat Go To).

FIREBALL FIX: boss_fireball.tscn (used by bosses, Gao, Pompon Flower, Tatanga,
Roketon, Yurarin Boo, Dragonzamasu, Nyololin) now faces the way it flies - its
art looks right, so Sprite Faces Left is off. Aimed and thrown shots face their
real direction too.
